Who we are

Alma Latina (formerly MexiCol) is the Hispanic Society of Kamloops' dance group, started by Mexican and Colombian women who dance the traditions they grew up with, and open to every Latin American country. Ranchera, norteña, son jarocho, sinaloense, and more: every costume tells where we come from, and every step invites you into the circle.

What we do

  • Performances at Fiesta Latina and Hispanic Heritage Month (years and dates to be confirmed).
  • Community and partner events (which ones is still to be confirmed).
  • Rehearsals open to newcomers (day and place to be confirmed).
  • Beginner workshops (a proposal, not yet scheduled).

Why it matters

Our dancers don't just entertain; they help sustain the Society. Every performance fills our events and helps raise funds that keep our cultural programs and family events going all year. Ticket sales, performance fees, and fundraising details will be published when the board confirms them. Every spin on stage helps a newcomer family feel at home.

Our goal: that dancing is also an income

Our goal is for Alma Latina dancers to earn an income for every event they perform. We are starting with a small, symbolic stipend per event, and our commitment is to grow it over time as donations, bookings, and sponsorships grow. Dancers give their art, and we want that art to be valued. The starting amount, how it is paid, and board approval are still to be confirmed.

Our styles

Mexican styles

Confirmed

Ranchera

Ranch-style joy: wide skirts that swirl, pride on stage, and songs everyone knows.

Confirmed

Norteña

The polka of northern Mexico: quick steps, boots, hats, denim, and accordion music.

Confirmed

Veracruzana (son jarocho)

White lace dresses with apron, fan, and shawl; white guayaberas with red bandanas; tarima zapateado to jarana and harp. La Bamba is the classic.

Confirmed

Sinaloense

The baile of the tambora band: full-foot stomping, quick waltzing, flowered blouses, wide skirts, and flowers in the hair; red bandanas and straw hats for the men.

Proposed — still to be confirmed by the Society

Jalisco / mariachi

Mexico's most iconic image: long ribboned dresses, embroidered charro suits, and the Jarabe Tapatío.

Proposed — still to be confirmed by the Society

Chihuahua

Polka and redova from the northern desert: fast spins, boots, and plaid shirts.

Proposed — still to be confirmed by the Society

Huasteca (huapango)

Strong zapateado with falsetto singing; embroidered quechquémitl for women, white guayaberas and palm hats for men.

Proposed — still to be confirmed by the Society

Oaxaca

Flor de Piña and the Valle sones: embroidered huipiles, ribbons, and bold colour.

Colombian styles (proposed, given the group's Colombian heritage)

Currulao

Mapalé

Pure energy from the coast: short dresses, fast moves, and drum rhythms.

Bambuco

Give our dancers their wings

Every skirt that spins on stage took hours of sewing, saving, and love. Your gift helps Alma Latina grow: costumes, dance shoes, travel to perform, and workshops that bring new dancers in. Your gift and every booking also help us fund a stipend for each dancer at every event, starting small and growing over time.
